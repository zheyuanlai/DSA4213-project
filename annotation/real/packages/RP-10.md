## Research package (exactly what the systems received)

## Research question
Does fine-tuning on self-generated concise reasoning (Method M) shorten LLM reasoning without reducing accuracy, compared with prompting and other fine-tuning approaches, and does the effect transfer across math datasets?

## Methods summary
Method M is a self-training recipe for shorter chain-of-thought. For each training question, the model samples reasoning paths while conditioned on 8 concise few-shot exemplars (here written by GPT-4o), plus paths from its default distribution; the shortest correct path per question is kept, and the model is fine-tuned on these paths. Method M-FS samples a single few-shot-conditioned path instead of best-of-N. Baselines: zero-shot prompting (including an explicit 'be concise' instruction), fine-tuning on external reasoning (GPT-4o chain-of-thought), naive best-of-N self-training without exemplars (16 paths), and Rational Metareasoning.

## Experimental setup
Five post-trained models (Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, DeepSeekMath-7B), fine-tuned separately for GSM8K and MATH; evaluation on the GSM8K test set and MATH500 with greedy decoding. Accuracy is parsed from final answers; length is the mean number of output tokens over all outputs, correct or not. Relative metrics divide by the zero-shot baseline prompt. Method M uses 16 few-shot-conditioned plus 16 default paths per question; the budget-matched variant uses 8 plus 8.

## Experiments and comparisons
- `exp1`: Concise-reasoning methods on GSM8K and MATH (T1)
  - comparison `exp1/cmp1`: Method M vs zero-shot baseline: accuracy and output length
  - comparison `exp1/cmp2`: Method M vs other length-reduction approaches (concise prompting, FT on GPT-4o CoT, naive best-of-N, Rational Metareasoning)
  - comparison `exp1/cmp3`: Method M full-budget vs budget-matched generation
- `exp2`: Cross-task generalization (T2)
  - comparison `exp2/cmp1`: In-domain vs out-of-domain relative accuracy and length
