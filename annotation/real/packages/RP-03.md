# Case RP-03

## Research package (exactly what the systems received)

## Research question
Does filtering self-generated preference data with Method M improve multilingual instruction-following over other filtering and data-scheduling baselines, consistently across two models and training rounds, without degrading general knowledge and reasoning accuracy?

## Methods summary
Method M is a preference-data filter for multilingual self-rewarding alignment. During each training round it tracks a moving-average gradient per language (kept in low-rank compressed form), removes conflicting components between languages by gradient projection, and sums them into a consensus direction. In the next round, each newly generated preference pair is scored by the cosine similarity between its gradient and this consensus direction, and the top 50% per language are kept for length-penalised DPO. Baselines keep the top 50% by other criteria (random, length margin, self-reward margin), use no filtering (Mult-Full), train each language separately (Mono), or reschedule languages (UAB, LCL).

## Experimental setup
Seed models: instruction-tuned Llama3-8B and Gemma2-2B. Training prompts: 1K AlpaGasus prompts machine-translated into 9 further languages (100 prompts per language in multilingual runs). Each round generates and self-scores four responses per prompt, builds preference pairs, filters, and trains one DPO epoch; five rounds. Instruction-following: head-to-head win rate versus the seed model on the aya evaluation suite, judged by GPT-4o. Alignment tax: 5-shot accuracy on Global MMLU and multilingual ARC Challenge. No significance tests or confidence intervals are reported.

## Experiments and comparisons
- `exp1`: Multilingual instruction-following after iterative self-rewarding with different preference-data filters (T1)
  - comparison `exp1/cmp1`: Method M vs Max-Reward (strongest other filter) in average win rate, Llama3-8B and Gemma2-2B
  - comparison `exp1/cmp2`: Method M vs unfiltered (Mult-Full), random filtering, and scheduling baselines (UAB, LCL)
  - comparison `exp1/cmp3`: Multilingual training (Mult-Full) vs monolingual training (Mono)
- `exp2`: Alignment tax on knowledge/reasoning benchmarks (T2)
  - comparison `exp2/cmp1`: Method M vs seed model and vs other filters on Global MMLU and ARC-C accuracy
