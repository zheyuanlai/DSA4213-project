## Research question
Does Method M detect that a benchmark was included in a language model's pretraining data more reliably than existing statistical membership tests across four benchmarks, and does it avoid flagging data the model never saw?

## Methods summary
Method M is a dataset-membership test for content owners. Before release, each document is rephrased several times by an instruction-tuned LLM, each rephrase carrying a text watermark generated with a different secret key. One rephrased version is published and the others are kept private. To test whether a model was trained on the published version, Method M compares the model's perplexity on the public rephrase with the average perplexity of the private rephrases of the same document, using a one-sided paired t-test (largest 5% of differences clipped). Ablations drop the pairing or the watermarking. Baselines are PaCoST and LLM DI.

## Experimental setup
Watermarked public and private rephrases of TriviaQA, ARC-C, MMLU and GSM8K (1,000 examples each) were generated with Llama3-70B-Instruct. Pythia-1B was continually pretrained on 6.7B tokens of OpenWebText mixed with the public versions; each benchmark appears exactly once and makes up less than 0.001% of the corpus. Every method returns a p-value for the null hypothesis of no membership. False positives are checked on an uncontaminated Pythia-1B and on held-out subsets watermarked with the same public key. The tables report one p-value per cell; no repeated training runs are reported for these results.

## Experiments and comparisons
- `exp1`: Intentional-contamination experiment: membership p-values on four benchmarks (T1)
  - comparison `exp1/cmp1`: Method M vs PaCoST and LLM DI (same/different): p-values on TriviaQA, ARC-C, MMLU, GSM8K
  - comparison `exp1/cmp2`: Method M vs its ablations without paired tests and without watermarking
- `exp2`: False-positive checks on data not seen in training (T2)
  - comparison `exp2/cmp1`: Method M p-values on an uncontaminated model and on held-out subsets of a contaminated model
