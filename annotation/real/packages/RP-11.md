## Research package (exactly what the systems received)

## Research question
Does Method M (and its calibrated variant Method M+) give larger certified robustness to edit-distance perturbations than fixed-rate smoothing baselines, consistently across text classification datasets, and at what cost in clean accuracy?

## Methods summary
Method M is a randomized-smoothing certification method for text classifiers against edit-distance attacks. At prediction time, tokens of the input are randomly deleted and a base classifier votes over many deleted copies; unlike the fixed-rate deletion baseline CERT-ED, Method M lets the deletion rate depend on input length (parameters: a lower bound on the deletion probability and a length constant), and the paper derives a matching certificate. Method M+ further sets the rate per length bin using an empirical calibration on the training set. RanMASK masks a fixed fraction of tokens and certifies only Hamming distance.

## Experimental setup
Base model: pretrained RoBERTa, fine-tuned with the same procedure for all smoothed models; NoSmooth is the plain classifier. Datasets: Yelp five-class sentiment (10,000 sampled test reviews), SpamAssassin spam detection, and LUN unreliable-news detection. Inference uses 1,000 Monte Carlo samples for prediction and 4,000 for certification. Metrics: clean accuracy; certified radius (CR, set to 0 for misclassified inputs); log10 cardinality of the certified region (CC); and a Wasserstein distance measuring how CR tracks input length.

## Experiments and comparisons
- `exp1`: Certified robustness vs clean accuracy on Yelp (5-class sentiment), SpamAssassin (spam) and LUN (unreliable news) (T1, T2)
  - comparison `exp1/cmp1`: Method M and Method M+ vs CERT-ED and RanMASK: mean certified radius and certified-region cardinality per dataset
  - comparison `exp1/cmp2`: Clean-accuracy cost of certification: smoothed models vs NoSmooth, and Method M/M+ vs CERT-ED
  - comparison `exp1/cmp3`: Adaptation to input length (Wasserstein distance) across methods
