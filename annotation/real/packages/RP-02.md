## Research package (exactly what the systems received)

## Research question
According to Method M, do persona instructions shift language-model outputs for a medical question, and does this sensitivity differ between larger and smaller models and across embedding choices?

## Methods summary
Method M audits whether an intervention on the prompt changes an LLM's output distribution. It samples k responses to the original prompt and k to the perturbed prompt, embeds them, and forms two sets of pairwise cosine similarities: within original responses (null) and between original and perturbed responses. The Jensen-Shannon divergence between these two similarity distributions is the effect size (omega); a permutation test over the pooled responses gives a p-value. The paper notes that a Bonferroni adjustment can be used when many perturbations are tested.

## Experimental setup
A healthcare question is posed to each model, with and without a prepended instruction 'Act as {role}'. Four roles are medical professions and four are unrelated (comedian, robot from the future, NeurIPS reviewer, child); the setup treats little shift for medical roles and a shift for unrelated roles as the expected pattern. Models: GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 (described as large) and several smaller open models. Default embedding: text-embedding-ada-002; an appendix repeats the persona experiment with three other embedding models. Each cell is a separate test.

## Experiments and comparisons
- `exp1`: Persona-perturbation audit across seven models with the default embedding (T1)
  - comparison `exp1/cmp1`: Medical vs non-medical personas: effect sizes and significance for the two large models (GPT-4, GPT-3.5)
  - comparison `exp1/cmp2`: Medical vs non-medical personas: effect sizes and significance for the smaller models
- `exp2`: Sensitivity of the audit to the choice of embedding model (T2)
  - comparison `exp2/cmp1`: Effect sizes and significance under Ada vs Jasper, Stella and Kalm embeddings
