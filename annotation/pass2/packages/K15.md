## Research package (exactly what the systems received)

## Research question
Is Marrowfield-ASR robust to additive background noise?

## Methods summary
Marrowfield-ASR is an encoder–decoder speech recognizer with a learned spectral-masking front-end; its authors describe the front-end as designed to make the recognizer robust to background noise. Ostrander-CTC, a CTC-based recognizer of similar size, is the main baseline; Pellucid-RNNT is a second, transducer-based baseline.

## Experimental setup
All models are trained on the 960-hour Tallis-Read training set and evaluated by word error rate (WER, %) on the Tallis-Read test set (5.4 hours of studio-quality read speech). Each model is trained with 3 seeds and WER is averaged over seeds. Noise study: each test utterance is mixed with background noise from the Ambrose-Noise collection (babble, street and café recordings, drawn uniformly) at signal-to-noise ratios of 20, 10 and 5 dB; Ambrose-Noise recordings are never used for training or tuning. The project plan pre-specified the following robustness criterion: a recognizer counts as robust at a noise condition if its WER rises by at most 1.5 absolute points above its WER on the original recordings.

## Experiments and comparisons
- `exp1`: Evaluation on the original Tallis-Read test recordings
  - comparison `exp1/cmp1`: Marrowfield-ASR vs Ostrander-CTC, WER
  - comparison `exp1/cmp2`: Marrowfield-ASR vs Pellucid-RNNT, WER
- `exp2`: Evaluation under additive background noise at 20, 10 and 5 dB SNR
  - comparison `exp2/cmp3`: WER increase over the original recordings at each SNR, per model
