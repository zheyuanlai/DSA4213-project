A sentence can understate its evidence in several ways, for example:
- certainty: it only describes a difference, or hedges ("may", "appears"), although a valid reported test or complete evidence supports an unqualified statement within the tested scope;
- coverage: it says "most", "some" or gives one example, although the evidence covers all evaluated cases;
- directness: it reports only a proxy (such as a count of computation steps), although the target quantity itself was measured under comparable conditions;
- attribution: it cites a source only for a direction or procedure, although the quoted passage reports a specific result.
